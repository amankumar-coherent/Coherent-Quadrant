Parameter Definitions

What each scoring parameter means for AI in Geospatial Imaging & Remote Sensing Market (defined specifically for this market).

X — Technology Capability
Geospatial AI Model Accuracy & Robustness
The precision, recall, and reliability of AI models in detecting and classifying geospatial features across diverse Indian terrains and conditions. Evidence includes published benchmarks, validation against ground truth, and performance metrics on Indian datasets.
Multi-Sensor & Multi-Resolution Data Fusion
Ability to integrate and analyze imagery from multiple sources such as optical, SAR, LiDAR, thermal, and hyperspectral at varying resolutions. Strength shown by seamless fusion outputs and support for Indian satellite and drone data formats.
Automated Feature Extraction & Change Detection
Capability to automatically identify and monitor changes in infrastructure, land use, agriculture, and defense targets over time. Evidence includes accuracy in detecting changes from multi-temporal imagery and real-time alerting features.
Scalable Cloud-Native Geospatial Processing
Architecture that handles large volumes of geospatial data efficiently on cloud platforms, with APIs and automation for Indian users. Strength indicated by processing speed, scalability tests, and integration with Indian cloud regions.
Domain-Specific AI Innovation & R&D
Ongoing research and development tailored to Indian geospatial challenges, such as monsoon monitoring, border surveillance, and urban planning. Evidence includes patents, publications, and novel AI techniques developed in-house.
Y — Business Capability
Indian Government & Defense Contract Wins
Number and value of contracts secured from Indian government agencies like ISRO, Survey of India, and Ministry of Defence. Evidence includes publicly announced deals and project awards.
Commercial Revenue & Growth in India
Annual revenue generated from Indian customers and year-over-year growth rate. Evidence includes financial statements, investor reports, and disclosed client wins.
Strategic Partnerships & Channel Network
Collaborations with Indian resellers, system integrators, and technology partners to expand market reach. Evidence includes named partners, joint go-to-market initiatives, and channel agreements.

Number of active Indian customers using the company's AI geospatial solutions and recurring revenue from subscriptions or licenses. Evidence includes customer counts, renewal rates, and case studies.
Funding, Ownership & Talent Scale
Total funding raised, ownership structure (e.g., Indian vs. foreign), and size of technical workforce in India. Evidence includes funding rounds, investor names, and employee headcount.

Research Methodology

This quadrant was compiled independently through structured secondary research, supplemented by primary research where available. It is not based on a survey or on vendor self-submission. The steps below describe how companies were identified, screened, evidenced and scored.

1
Market Definition
The market scope and the type of company that qualifies were defined first. Five parameters were then set for each axis, each with a written definition: the X axis measures core capability (products, services or technology) and the Y axis measures business capability.
→
2
Secondary and Primary Research
Secondary research covered company websites, filings, trade directories, industry publications and news. Primary research drew on B2B marketplace listings, where available, to confirm company offerings. Coverage was extended region by region until no new qualifying companies emerged.
→
3
Screening
Each candidate was screened against the market's inclusion criteria and required commercial role. Distributors, resellers, consultancies, contract manufacturers and media names were excluded, and duplicates consolidated. Companies whose main business is an adjacent segment are listed but not placed on the chart.
→
4
Evidence Compilation
Every screened company was assessed on all ten parameters; for chart companies, evidence was recorded parameter by parameter. Claims resting only on a company's own materials are reported as company-reported, partner technology is credited to the partnership, and employee-review sites are not used as customer evidence.
→
5
Scoring
Each parameter is rated 0–100 against its written definition, based on the evidence available. Ratings are analyst assessments under this methodology, not independently audited figures. X and Y are the simple average of the five parameter ratings on each axis; overall is the average of X and Y.
→
6
Normalization & Selection
X and Y scores are rescaled to a 65–100 range across all companies assessed, preserving their rank order. The chart shows an equal number of companies from each of the four quadrants, with boundaries set at the median. All other companies are listed in the table below the chart.

Scoring inputs: each axis is built from five market-specific parameters, defined in the Parameter Definitions section. All parameters are equally weighted.
