Parameter Definitions

What each parameter measures and the evidence that shows strength.

X — Product Capability
  1. Dressing Material Innovation
    The ability to develop advanced dressings (e.g., foams, hydrogels, alginates, antimicrobials) with superior exudate management, moisture balance, and biocompatibility. Strength is evidenced by patented formulations, published comparative studies, and a pipeline of next-generation materials.
  2. Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (NPWT) Device Performance
    The technical sophistication of NPWT systems, including portable designs, pressure control accuracy, battery life, and integration with smart monitoring. Strength is shown by device certifications, user feedback, and clinical outcomes data on wound healing rates.
  3. Biologic and Skin Substitute Portfolio
    The range and efficacy of biologic products such as growth factors, collagen matrices, and cultured skin substitutes for hard-to-heal wounds. Strength is demonstrated by FDA/EMA approvals, peer-reviewed studies, and breadth of indications covered.
  4. Clinical Evidence Generation
    The commitment to conducting and publishing randomized controlled trials, real-world evidence, and health economics studies that validate product performance. Strength is measured by number of high-impact publications, clinical trial registrations, and inclusion in treatment guidelines.
  5. Manufacturing Scalability and Quality Systems
    The capacity to produce advanced wound care products at scale while maintaining sterility, consistency, and regulatory compliance. Strength is evidenced by ISO 13485 certifications, FDA inspections, and ability to meet global demand without supply disruptions.
Y — Business Capability
  1. Hospital and Provider Network Reach
    The extent to which a company's products are adopted by hospitals, wound care clinics, and long-term care facilities. Strength is shown by the number of contracted healthcare systems, group purchasing organization agreements, and formulary listings.
  2. Revenue and Reimbursement Strength
    The company's sales performance and ability to secure favorable reimbursement from public and private payers. Strength is evidenced by reported revenue growth, gross margins, and percentage of products covered by major insurance plans.
  3. Global Distribution and Sales Infrastructure
    The breadth of a company's direct sales force and distributor network across key regions. Strength is measured by number of countries served, local subsidiaries, and partnerships with regional distributors.
  4. Strategic Partnerships and Acquisitions
    The ability to form alliances or acquire complementary businesses to expand product offerings or market access. Strength is shown by announced deals, joint ventures, and integration of acquired technologies into the portfolio.
  5. Installed Base and Consumable Pull-Through
    The number of NPWT devices or other durable equipment placed in hospitals and clinics, which drives recurring sales of consumables. Strength is evidenced by installed base figures, replacement cycle data, and consumable revenue per device.
